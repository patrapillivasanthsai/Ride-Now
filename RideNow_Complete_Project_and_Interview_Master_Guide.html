Imagine you want to travel from your college to a coffee shop. You open your phone, tap "Book Auto", and in 30 seconds, a nearby driver accepts your trip, drives to your location, picks you up, drops you off, and you pay ₹60 in Cash or UPI.

RideNow is the complete full-stack software brain that makes this entire experience happen seamlessly in real-time. Just like Rapido, Uber, or Ola, RideNow connects three distinct groups of users:

🗄️ Chapter 3: Database Schema & Entity Blueprint

RideNow uses PostgreSQL with Prisma ORM (`backend/prisma/schema.prisma`). Here is what every table stores:

  • User & AdminStaff: Core authentication credentials (email, bcrypt password hash, role: CUSTOMER, DRIVER, ADMIN) and mobile push notification tokens (fcmToken).
  • Customer: Passenger profile with phone number, saved Stripe customer ID, trusted emergency contacts, and payment preferences.
  • Driver, Vehicle & DriverLocation: Captain verification details (license number, isApproved, status: OFFLINE | ONLINE | BUSY), registered vehicle (make, model, license plate, strict type: BIKE | AUTO | CAB), and live GPS coordinates.
  • Ride & RideStatusHistory: Complete trip ledger (pickup/dropoff coordinates, authoritative ₹ INR fare, vehicle type, distance, duration, assigned driver ID, and status) with an immutable audit timeline.
  • RideDeclinedDriver: Tracks drivers who declined a specific ride, isolating them so they are not re-prompted while the engine rolls over to the next captain.
  • Payment: Financial transaction ledger (amount in ₹ INR, method: CASH | UPI | CARD, status: PENDING | AUTHORIZED | COMPLETED | FAILED | REFUNDED, provider, gateway transaction ID).
  • Pricing: Dynamic fare rates per vehicle class (base fare, per-km rate, per-minute rate, minimum fare).

⚡ Chapter 4: Core Workflows & Algorithms

1. Authoritative Fare Estimation

When a passenger selects locations, the backend calculates the fare authoritatively:

  • Haversine Formula: Calculates straight-line great-circle distance between coordinates in kilometers.
  • OSRM Road Geometry: Computes realistic driving route distance and travel duration in minutes.
  • INR Formula: Total Fare = Base Fare + (Distance in km × Per Km Rate) + (Duration in mins × Per Minute Rate).
  • Security Rule: The frontend never determines the price. The backend calculates and validates all pricing against the Pricing table.

2. The Automatic Dispatch & Driver Matching Engine

When a ride is ready for matching (Cash booking or Online payment hold):

  1. Strict 5-Point Eligibility Check: Captain must be (1) isApproved === true, (2) isSuspended === false, (3) status === ONLINE, (4) vehicle.type === ride.vehicleType (Strict matching: Bike → Bike, Auto → Auto, Cab → Cab), and (5) Captain has no ongoing active trip.
  2. Proximity Sorting: Filters out any driver who already declined this ride, and sorts remaining eligible drivers by distance to pickup (closest captain first).
  3. Targeted Alert: Sends an instant WebSocket event (incoming_ride_request) to the closest matching captains and dispatches an FCM push notification.
  4. Automatic Roll-Over: If the driver declines, the decline is saved in RideDeclinedDriver, and the engine immediately dispatches to the next candidate.

3. Atomic Concurrency Lock (Preventing Double-Booking)

To ensure two drivers tapping "Accept" at the exact same millisecond never both get assigned, RideNow executes an atomic transaction with conditional write locking:

Database Concurrency Logic
updateMany({ where: { id: rideId, status: 'SEARCHING_DRIVER' }, data: { status: 'DRIVER_ASSIGNED', driverId: winningDriverId } })
PostgreSQL serializes the write. The first driver updates 1 row and succeeds (HTTP 200). The second driver matches 0 rows, throws CONCURRENT_MODIFICATION_DETECTED, and receives HTTP 409 Conflict.

🎯 Chapter 5: Master Interview Questions & Answers (30+ Scenarios)

Section 1: Architecture & System Design

Q1 Can you give an elevator pitch / high-level summary of RideNow?

RideNow is a production-grade, real-time on-demand ride-hailing and fleet management platform modeled after Rapido and Uber. It features a TypeScript/Node.js backend with PostgreSQL and Prisma ORM, Socket.IO WebSockets, a React 18 customer web app, a React Native driver mobile app, and an 11-module admin operations portal. Key engineering accomplishments include an autonomous proximity-based driver dispatch engine with strict vehicle matching, atomic concurrency locking to eliminate race conditions, and segregated payment pre-authorization holds vs cash-at-completion lifecycles.

Q2 Why did you choose PostgreSQL + Prisma over MongoDB for this project?
  • ACID Transactions: Ride booking and driver acceptance require atomic multi-table updates. If a driver accepts a ride, updating the ride status, driver duty status, and status history must either all succeed or all roll back together.
  • Relational Integrity: Relational foreign keys prevent corrupted data (e.g. deleting a user automatically cascades or cleans up related driver/customer entities cleanly).
  • Complex Multi-Condition Queries: Finding online, approved drivers with a specific vehicle type who haven't declined a ride is vastly more efficient in relational databases using composite indexes.
Q3 How does real-time communication work in RideNow?

We use Socket.IO with a hierarchical room architecture:

  • Role Rooms: role:DRIVER is used to broadcast new available trips to online drivers.
  • Entity Rooms: ride:{rideId} and user:{userId} are used for targeted events (e.g. sending an incoming ride pop-up directly to the closest captain, or sending status updates to the passenger).
  • Resilient Polling Fallback: If a mobile driver's network drops packets, the client uses intelligent short-polling (every 3-5 seconds) to ensure synchronization without locking the UI.
Q4 How would you scale RideNow to 1 Million active rides per day?
  • Stateless API Clustering: Scale Node.js instances horizontally behind an Application Load Balancer / Nginx.
  • Redis Socket Adapter: Use @socket.io/redis-adapter so WebSocket events broadcast across multiple server instances.
  • Geospatial Caching: Use Redis Geohashing (GEOADD, GEORADIUS) or PostgreSQL PostGIS for sub-millisecond proximity queries across 100,000+ online drivers.
  • Distributed Message Queues: Move dispatch iterations and push notifications to background workers using BullMQ / RabbitMQ.

Section 2: Concurrency, Race Conditions & Database Transactions

Q5 How do you prevent two drivers from accepting the same ride simultaneously?

We use an atomic conditional database update inside prisma.$transaction:

tx.ride.updateMany({ where: { id: rideId, status: 'SEARCHING_DRIVER' }, data: { status: 'DRIVER_ASSIGNED', driverId } })

PostgreSQL row-level locking ensures that Driver A's write updates 1 row (count = 1). When Driver B's request runs a millisecond later, the status is already DRIVER_ASSIGNED, so 0 rows match (count = 0). The backend catches this, throws CONCURRENT_MODIFICATION_DETECTED, and returns HTTP 409 Conflict to Driver B.

Q6 How do you guarantee that a driver is never assigned to two active rides at once?
  • Query Guard: In DispatchService, drivers with ongoing active rides (status IN [DRIVER_ASSIGNED, DRIVER_ARRIVING, DRIVER_ARRIVED, RIDE_STARTED]) are strictly excluded from dispatch queries.
  • Atomic Duty Switch: When a driver accepts a ride, their duty state is set to BUSY in the same database transaction.
  • API Guard: If a driver calls the accept endpoint while their status is BUSY, the API rejects the request with HTTP 400 DRIVER_BUSY.
Q7 What happens when a driver declines a ride request?
  1. The driver calls POST /api/driver/rides/:id/decline.
  2. The backend upserts a record in the RideDeclinedDriver table ({ rideId, driverId, reason }).
  3. The request is dismissed from that driver's mobile screen.
  4. The backend triggers DispatchService.dispatchRide(rideId), which excludes all declined driver IDs and dispatches the request to the next nearest eligible captain.

Section 3: Payments & Financial Safety

Q8 Why did you separate Payment Status from Ride Status?

In a ride-hailing app, money and physical vehicle movement represent two distinct lifecycles:

  • Ride Status: REQUESTED → SEARCHING_DRIVER → DRIVER_ASSIGNED → RIDE_STARTED → RIDE_COMPLETED.
  • Payment Status: PENDING → AUTHORIZED → COMPLETED → REFUNDED.
  • Cash Clarity: For Cash rides, Ride.status = SEARCHING_DRIVER while Payment.status = PENDING (labeled "Cash: Pay directly to captain at destination"). This prevents confusing passengers with false payment errors while clearly tracking receivables.
Q9 How does the Stripe Online Payment Pre-authorization hold work?

We use a Two-Step Capture workflow:

  1. Hold: When booking with a Card, we call Stripe's paymentIntents.create with capture_method: 'manual'. This places an authorization hold on the customer's card without charging them immediately.
  2. Dispatch Trigger: Only once the hold is confirmed (status: requires_capture) does the ride transition to SEARCHING_DRIVER to start matching captains.
  3. Capture: When the trip reaches RIDE_COMPLETED, the backend captures the held funds. If cancelled before driver assignment, the hold is voided with zero transaction fees.

Section 4: Security, Authentication & CORS

Q10 What was the CORS Preflight issue and how did you resolve it?

Problem: When the Admin Portal (port 5174) or Customer Web (port 5173) sent PATCH/POST requests with custom Authorization headers to the Backend (port 3000), browsers sent an initial OPTIONS preflight request. The Express auth middleware intercepted the OPTIONS request and returned 401 Unauthorized because browsers do not attach Bearer tokens to preflights.

Solution: In auth.middleware.ts, we added if (req.method === 'OPTIONS') return next(); and registered a universal CORS preflight handler returning 200/204 with explicit Access-Control-Allow-Methods and Access-Control-Allow-Origin.
